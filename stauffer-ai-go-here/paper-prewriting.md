# Paper topic ideas

The paper has to be on microeconomics or macroeconomics. These are the topics I'm considering.

The major question behind all of these is about demand uncertainty: will customers do what we want, and spend money? I want to know whether there's a way to have real comfort that people will buy tickets, coffees, or lessons.

## Topic 1: Are we preying on artist desperation, and is there a better way?

**Field:** Microeconomics

**Question I want to answer:** Are society, the market, and the coaching world preying on the desperation of artists trying to work as professionals, and is there a better way? By "preying" I mean the price depends on how badly the buyer wants a result instead of what is actually delivered, and the buyer can't check beforehand whether the result will come.

**Why it interests me:** I'm an MBA student trying to use my soft skills of teaching and project thinking to build a career that supplements my playing career. I've been pitched $3k+ business programs from multiple sources, and I'm interested in the ones that charge that much without really delivering results. I'm also thinking about creating a microcredential in solo artistic entrepreneurship at the university, so I want to know what a better, more honest model would look like. My underlying question is how much of an entrepreneurial career is within a person's control, and how much is luck.

**Economic concepts I'd use:** Mostly I think the problem is that there are too many coaches and too many desperate customers, and no way to compare what's being sold. Because the coaches can't be compared and the customers don't know what's fair, the prices don't come from supply and demand, they come from whatever the coach decides to charge. I'd use ideas like price discovery, product differentiation, information asymmetry, price discrimination, and willingness to pay.

**What I'd need to find out / data I could use:** I'd review the sales pages of group and cohort coaching programs, and I'm willing to send research emails to ask about price and what's included. I want to find out what clients are actually paying for: skills, success, or a network. I think cohort programs expand the coach's profit margins while reducing the one-on-one time each student gets, so I'd compare price, group size, and hours promised. The data would be pitches that I or people I know have received, plus information I can gather online.

**What a better way might look like:** I don't know yet, and figuring that out is what the paper is about.

## Topic 2: How does a big local event affect small and arts businesses in a small town?

**Field:** Macroeconomics (local or regional impact)

**Question I want to answer:** When a major act comes to a small town, like the Rolling Stones coming to Charlottesville, Virginia, what happens to the small and local arts businesses nearby?

**Why it interests me:** I'm drawn to the small-business angle, and I'm hopeful that a small business's outcome can be less luck-based than it feels. A big event is a demand boost that's visible well ahead of time, so it's a chance to see how owners who plan for it can get calmer control over what happens to them.

**Economic concepts I'd use:** Economic impact and multiplier effects, demand shocks that can be predicted in advance, and whether the money stays local.

**What I'd need to find out / data I could use:** Local tax data if it's available, and short interviews or emails with business owners.

## Topic 3: Why do small businesses fail?

**Field:** Microeconomics

**Question I want to answer:** Why do small, public-facing, service-based businesses (arts shops, coffee shops) fail, and how much of it is bad luck versus things owners can control? I count a business as failing when it stops for a reason like running out of cash, burning out, or not making a profit.

**Why it interests me:** My hunch is that they fail because of bad luck, and I'd like to discover that this isn't true. Luck is the main thing I'm interested in about business, and I want to believe that an owner can have more control than it seems.

**Economic concepts I'd use:** I think all of this comes down to whether customers will spend money, so I'd look at demand uncertainty, and ways to reduce it like pre-sales, memberships and repeat customers, along with cash flow, break-even and survivorship bias.

**What I'd need to find out / data I could use:** I'd ask a few local owners how they know customers will show up, and compare that with how long their businesses lasted. I could also look at public survival statistics for small businesses.
