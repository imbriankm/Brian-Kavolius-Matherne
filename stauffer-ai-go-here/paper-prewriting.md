## Topic 1: Are we preying on artist desperation, and is there a better way?

**Field:** Microeconomics

**Question I want to answer:** Are society, the market, and the coaching world preying on the desperation of artists trying to work as professionals, and is there a better way? By "preying" I mean the price depends on how badly the buyer wants a result instead of what is actually delivered, and the buyer can't check beforehand whether the result will come.

**Why it interests me:** I'm an MBA student trying to use my soft skills of teaching and project thinking to build a career that supplements my playing career. I've been pitched $3k+ business programs from multiple sources, and I'm interested in the ones that charge that much without really delivering results. I'm also thinking about creating a microcredential in solo artistic entrepreneurship at the university, so I want to know what a better, more honest model would look like. My underlying question is how much of an entrepreneurial career is within a person's control, and how much is luck.

**Economic concepts I'd use:** Mostly I think the problem is that there are too many coaches and too many desperate customers, and no way to compare what's being sold. Because the coaches can't be compared and the customers don't know what's fair, the prices don't come from supply and demand, they come from whatever the coach decides to charge. I'd use ideas like price discovery, product differentiation, information asymmetry, price discrimination, and willingness to pay.

**What I'd need to find out / data I could use:** I'd review the sales pages of group and cohort coaching programs, and I'm willing to send research emails to ask about price and what's included. I want to find out what clients are actually paying for: skills, success, or a network. I think cohort programs expand the coach's profit margins while reducing the one-on-one time each student gets, so I'd compare price, group size, and hours promised. The data would be pitches that I or people I know have received, plus information I can gather online.

**What a better way might look like:** I don't know yet, and figuring that out is what the paper is about.
