# <Engagement> — engagement brief

## The problem
My farm needs to decide how many of the three crops (tomatoes, carrots, mesclun) to plant this year. The farm has 64 beds and the ability to hire four workers. The starting budget is $20,000. There are 64 beds to plant in, and the average price of a bed, just in fertilizer, is just over $700. This means it is easy to use the whole budget without using all available beds. The budget is the limitation, not the beds. Tomatoes have a large revenue of $8800, but requires a larger labor force and has a greater diminishing returns rate. Carrots have the smallest revenue and the least costs. Mesclun has a slightly larger revenue than carrots, and is the most efficient in terms of diminishing returns. The challenge will be to use the budget appropriately to maximize profits, when considering labor costs, fertilizer costs, "diminishing returns rate", and the hopeful profit. If the wrong choice is made, there could be losses, not enough profits, or a failure to have enough labor to do the harvesting.

## What I am assuming
I'm assuming all of the information I've been given is correct.


## Hypothesis
I expect that the best choice will be to plant mostly carrots, then mesclun, with tomatoes being last. I think this because carrots carry the lowest costs. I do not have to consider the amount of beds available, because due to my budget, I will be unable to fill them all anyway. 
"I expect X because Y." Real quantities, and the mechanism you think decides
it. You are not judged on being right. A hedged prediction that would survive
any outcome is the only kind that is worthless.
