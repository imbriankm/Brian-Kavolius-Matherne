# Perfect competition — recommendation

Dear Mrs. Guenivere J. Interdierchematter,

I hope this note finds you in good spirits and in strong health. I'm writing to report on my analysis for your farm. If you read no further, please mark my ultimate recommendation that you plant for this season the following distribution:

- 10 Tomato Beds
- 20 Carrot Beds
- 30 Mesclun Beds.

Below, you'll find the reasoning for these recommendations, based on an analysis of diminishing returns on repeated planting, your fixed costs, your variable costs, labor concerns, and considerations for future success. Of course, should you wish to ask me further questions, I urge you to write to me via email rather than your preferred carrier pigeon. The stench is becoming irksome.

## Basis

It's best to view these crops in two parts. Tomatoes are a high revenue item, but their profitability fails after the 10th bed. The carrot and mesclun beds don't behave in the same way, and you may plant as many as your beds will allow. It is my recommendation that you start here when considering adding beds for expansion in your future. I trust that Lord McLegitichopper will work with you for the necessary preparations.

I know you have expressed concern about your fixed $20,000 costs, which includes your annual tribute to the local Grim. Please be assured that your farm has the potential to be strongly profitable. If you follow the recommended plan, you should maintain over $42k in profit.

## The Judgement

Moving forward, I recommend you consider the following. First, please consider retiring from your farm work. That you can hire temporary workers for half the price of your own labor means that you may still find profit while attending to other matters, such as the unfortunate incident involving the dolls. My condolences for your loss. Additionally, adding beds to your farm through land acquisition or another more invasive technique may provide you profit generating opportunities in the future.

If you examine the model I've included with this communiqué, you will find that the temporary workers are not only half as expensive as your labor, but that the temporary workers retain over 1200 hours of availability, after yours are expended. Replacing your 720 hours with a temporary laborer would reduce the labor costs for those hours by $12,500. You could use this extra resource to fund the bed expansion I describe, or to repair the damage to your wagon caused by the wayward being with an unspeakable name.

Additionally, you may calculate, using the model, that another carrot bed will add $352.49 of profit and one more mesclun bed will add $246.47 of profit.

## Factors of the Future

Of course, as the illusion of time passes, all things become murky. If crop prices change, worker labor prices, or a change in the current structure of your business were to change, these recommendations may become irrelevant.
